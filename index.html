About

I completed my BSc and MSc in computer science at the Technion, where I worked with Prof. Alex Bronstein on machine learning for scientific applications. I then joined the PhD program at ISTA, which I left to pursue PhD opportunities aligned with my research interests.

My research so far has focused on generative modeling and probabilistic inference in biology and physics, including protein structure modeling under experimental constraints and learning from correlated observations. Working on these problems drew me toward understanding models themselves: how they represent knowledge, how they use it in computation, and how their internal mechanisms produce behavior.

My current interests center on mechanistic interpretability and the science of AI. I want to understand the representational and computational principles in trained models, and how these principles emerge from the interaction between training dynamics and architecture. I am drawn to work that connects mathematical reasoning with empirical analysis of trained models.

Outside of research, I enjoy reading books, snowboarding, and surfing. If an activity comes with no risk of breaking a leg, it is probably not worth my time. Except for books. Books are awesome.
